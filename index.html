Two halves, meeting at the line list

Authoring

Add a work and the tool proposes the ones that usually follow, ranked by where the suggestion came from — a named build-up, a sequencing rule, or plain co-occurrence — each printed with its evidence and sample size.

Pricing

An estimate is data, not a folder of workbooks. Versions are immutable and each carries a reason. Rate books are dated and never edited, so an old quote still computes and inflation is measurable.

From drawings

A drawing set carries its own quantified finish schedule: every material with its mark, its location and its area. The tool reads that schedule, maps each finish to a norm code, and proposes priced lines — each citing the page and the exact figure it came from.

How much of a quote is derivable that way was an open question, so it was measured against one completed fit-out project's issued quote:

  • 34%of the works the estimator actually priced, recovered from the drawings alone
  • 3quantities that matched exactly, to the square metre
  • 0quantities invented — a figure not printed on the page is refused

The other two thirds are substrate, reinforcement, preparation and site work — what a finish schedule never shows. That is what the build-up library is for. Neither half reaches a full quote alone; together they plausibly do.

What it refuses to do

  • No language model in the arithmetic or the ranking. A model reads and matches; it never counts and never names a code. Every figure traces to a norm row, a material price or a past estimate line.
  • It never invents a rate. Where no norm exists, the line is emitted unpriced carrying the reason, not quietly filled with a plausible number.
  • A typed correction is evidence. It prices the line, and it is recorded dated and attributed, so using the tool is what stops the masters going stale.
  • Every number it reports is verifiable. A quantity read off a drawing must appear on that page; a measured one can be drawn back onto the drawing that produced it.
